Textbook

TEXTBOOK: Introduction to Analysis, by Edward D. Gaughan, 5th Edition, American Mathematical Society.

Learning Outcomes

Students will master concepts of real analysis and learn how to do proofs. Successful students in Analysis I will become knowledgeable about and will master concepts of real analysis and will improve their ability to write and read mathematical proofs, particularly those related to the least upper bound axiom, compactness, sequences, continuity, uniform continuity, dierentiation, Riemann integration, and the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Reading

Reading the textbook in advance of the lecture is strongly encouraged. Benefits of this preparation include obtaining a familiarity with the terminology and concepts that will be encountered, being able to formulate questions about the parts of the presentation that you do not understand, and having a chance to review the skills and techniques that will be needed to apply the new concepts. Students should check Blackboard or the class website frequently for announcements and course documents such as homework assignments, solutions to homework and exam problems, and handouts.

Grading Policy

Course grades will be determined from student performance on exams and homework. Homework will be assigned for each section, and will be collected regularly (details about this will be given as appropriate). One lowest homework score will be dropped from homework grade calculation. Late homework will not be accepted, and no make-ups for missing homework. There will be three exams: two midterm exams and a final exam. The two in-class exams, which are indicated on the syllabus above, will be given during the time normally used for lecture. Each of two midterm exams test only the material covered since the previous exam. In contrast the final exam, which will be given during the week of final exams, is a cumulative exam. Reason for missing an exam must be properly documented and any missed exam must be made up within a week in general. The grades for the course are determined as follows:
