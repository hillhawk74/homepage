Textbook and Course Outline

TEXTBOOK : Differential Equations and Their Applications (4th ed., Springer), by Martin Braun.

Reading the material in advance of the lecture is strongly encouraged.

Course content: Differential equations is the language of science. Many basic scientific laws express the change in one quantity in terms of the values of other quantities and form some kind of differential equations, and then the challenge is to understand the "solution" of the system. In this course, we will discuss differential equations of the first and second order, linear systems of ordinary differential equations, and their elementary qualitative properties.

Grading Policy

Course grades will be determined from student performance on exams and quizzes. There will be weekly 20-minute quizzes and three exams: two 75-minute in-class exams and a final exam. All quizzes will be given on Thursday, but the instructor reserves the right to give a quiz at any time. The two lowest quiz scores will be dropped and no make-up quizzes will be given. The two in-class exams, which are indicated on the syllabus above, will be given during the hour normally used for lecture. Each of these exams will test only the material covered since the previous exam. In contrast the final exam, which will be given during the week of final exams, will be a cummulative exam. Reason for missing an exam must be properly documented and any missed exam must be made up within a week. The grades for the course are determined as follows:

 Exam 1  25%
 Exam 2  25%
 Final Exam  35%
 Quizzes  15%
-----
 Total  100%

A straight scale will be used so that A's, B's C's and D's correspond to the percent ranges 100-90, 90-80, 80-70 and 70-60 respectively. Plus grading will be used. For example:

90-100:A 86-89: B+ 80-85: B 76-79: C+
70-75: C 66-69: D+ 60-65: D 0-59: F

For graduate students who take this course, additional projects will be assigned for deciding their final grades. Homework will be assigned for each section. You are expected to work all of these problems. Althought not collected in general, these homework assignments are an essential part of the course for learning and understanding the course material. They should be thought of as required for success in the course.

Attendance and Academic Honesty

Attendance at every class meeting is important and expected. Students missing more than 10% of the class meetings (4 times) can have their grade lowered. Cheating and plagiarism will not be tolerated. Violations of this policy will be dealt with according to University guidelines.
